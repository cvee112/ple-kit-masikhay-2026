Step by step

How to apply via LERIS

All applications go through the PRC's Licensure Examination and Registration Information System (LERIS). First-timers must then appear personally at the PRC office on their appointment date.

  1. Create your LERIS account

    Go to online.prc.gov.ph → Register. Use an email you'll keep long-term (you'll use this same account for your license later). Enter your name, birthdate, and details exactly as they appear on your PSA birth certificate — mismatches cause failed transactions and delayed appointments.

  2. Complete your profile & upload your photo

    Fill in personal, educational, and internship information. Upload the square 2×2 digital photo (white background, collared attire, ears visible, no glasses). If the uploader rejects it, re-crop to an exact square and check the file size.

  3. Select the transaction

    From your profile: Select Transaction → Examination → Physician. Choose the October 2026 exam and your preferred testing center (e.g., Manila/NCR).

  4. Book your PRC appointment

    Pick a PRC office and an available appointment date for document submission. NCR slots go quickly — book the earliest date you can be ready for. Note your appointment details and reference number.

  5. Pay the examination fee — ₱900

    Pay through the PRC-authorized channels shown in LERIS (LandBank online, GCash/Maya, and other listed partners). Keep the receipt/screenshot. LERIS usually reflects payment within a day or two — if a transaction fails, don't pay twice immediately; wait for the system to confirm first.

  6. Print your application form

    Once payment is confirmed, download and print the system-generated application form. Print at home to avoid queueing for printing services at PRC.

The golden ticket

Appointment day → Notice of Admission

  1. Go to PRC on your scheduled date

    Bring the complete set: printed application form, all original documents + photocopies, photos, valid ID, payment proof, documentary stamps, and envelopes. Incomplete requirements will not be accepted.

  2. Document verification

    PRC staff review and verify your documents against your online record. Originals of the TOR, diploma, internship certificate, and GMCs are inspected; keep everything organized in your envelope in the order listed.

  3. Receive your Notice of Admission (NOA)

    If everything checks out, you get your NOA and Program of Examination. The NOA is your entry pass on exam day — photograph it, photocopy it, and keep the original safe.

  4. Check your room assignment

    Room assignments are posted on the PRC website and official pages a few days before each exam weekend. Visit your building/room in advance so you know the commute, gates, and floor.

Beware of fixers. PRC repeatedly warns against individuals claiming PRC connections who offer to "facilitate" applications or "guarantee" passing. Report them; never hand over documents or money. Results are final — there is no re-checking of test papers.
Know the battlefield

Exam structure & coverage

The PLE runs over four days across two weekends and covers 12 subjects, each in multiple-choice format. To pass, you need a general average of at least 75% with no subject rating below 50%.

ClusterSubjects
Basic sciencesAnatomy & Histology · Physiology · Biochemistry · Microbiology & Parasitology
Bridging sciencesPathology · Pharmacology & Therapeutics · Legal Medicine, Ethics & Medical Jurisprudence
Clinical sciencesMedicine · Surgery (incl. Ophthalmology & ORL) · Obstetrics & Gynecology · Pediatrics & Nutrition · Preventive Medicine & Public Health

The exact per-day subject schedule is in the official Program of Examination for October 2026, released on the PRC website closer to the exam — check it once posted, since day groupings can shift between administrations.

From your batchmates & those who came before

PLE review strategy

1 · Structure the runway (roughly 3 phases)

  • Foundation (first ~6 weeks): full pass through high-yield reviewers per subject. Don't chase completeness — chase coverage. Basic sciences fade fastest post-internship; front-load Anatomy, Physio, Biochem, Micro.
  • Consolidation (next ~4–5 weeks): second pass focused on weak subjects flagged by your practice scores. Convert notes into recall material (flashcards, one-page summaries).
  • Simulation (final ~3 weeks): timed, full-length mock exams under exam conditions. Review errors > reviewing notes. Taper the last 3 days — sleep is a performance intervention.

2 · Practice questions are the main event

Passive rereading feels productive and isn't. Retrieval practice — answering questions, then studying the rationales for both right and wrong answers — is the highest-yield use of an hour. Track per-subject accuracy so your remaining time flows to your weakest subjects, not your favorite ones.

Remember the passing math: 75% average, nothing below 50%. A weak subject you ignore is the single most dangerous thing on the board — it can sink an otherwise strong average. Shore up floors before raising ceilings.

3 · Twelve subjects, one calendar

Interleave rather than block for weeks on one subject: two subjects a day (one basic, one clinical) beats a month of Medicine followed by cramming Biochem. Spaced repetition on the same material at widening intervals is what makes it stick to October.

4 · Between the two weekends

You get a week between exam days. Resist post-mortems on the first weekend's papers — you can't change them, and dwelling costs you the second weekend. Use the gap week for the remaining subjects only.

5 · Take care of the examinee

Review with people who steady you. Eat, run, sleep. The batch that carried each other through duty nights at PGH can carry each other through this — check in on your review group, and ask for help early if you're struggling. Lakas ng loob includes knowing when to rest.

The other side

After the exam

  1. Results release

    Target release is October 16, 2026 — PRC has recently released PLE results within about four working days of the last exam day. Verify your individual rating through the PRC's online Verification of Rating once posted.

  2. Initial registration (getting your license)

    Passers register online via LERIS for the issuance of the Certificate of Registration and Professional Identification Card. Based on the March 2026 cycle, requirements included: the duly accomplished Oath Form (Panunumpa ng Propesyonal), your NOA (for identification), 2 passport-size photos (white background, complete name tag), 2 sets of documentary stamps, and 1 short brown envelope. You personally sign the Roster of Registered Professionals.

  3. Oathtaking

    The PRC and the Board of Medicine announce the oathtaking ceremony date and venue after results are released. Then it's official: M.D., Lic. No. ______.
